THE NECKLACE

Title : The Necklace
Author : Guy de Maupassant
Genre : Short story,Realism
Published : 1884
Theme : Appearance vs. reality, vanity, materialism
Characters : Mathilde Loisel, Monsieur Loisel, Madame Forestier

Mathilde Loisel was a beautiful young woman who was born into a poor family. She married Monsieur Loisel, a simple clerk who worked in the Ministry of Education. Although her husband was satisfied with their modest life, Mathilde was not.

Mathilde always dreamed of living a rich and luxurious life. She imagined herself in a beautiful house, surrounded by expensive furniture, servants, fine clothes, jewellery, and delicious food. She was unhappy because she believed that she deserved a much better life.

One day, Monsieur Loisel came home with an invitation to a grand evening party at the Ministry. He was very happy because he thought Mathilde would enjoy the opportunity. Instead, she became upset because she had nothing beautiful to wear.

Her husband had been saving money to buy a gun, but when he saw how unhappy she was, he gave her the money to buy a suitable dress. Mathilde bought a beautiful dress, but she was still unhappy because she had no jewellery to wear.

Her husband suggested that she visit her wealthy friend, Madame Forestier, and borrow some jewellery.

Mathilde went to Madame Forestier's house. Her friend opened her jewellery box and allowed Mathilde to choose anything she liked. Mathilde looked at several pieces of jewellery and finally found a beautiful diamond necklace.

She borrowed the necklace and went home happily.

On the evening of the party, Mathilde looked beautiful and elegant. Everyone admired her. She was happy because she finally felt like the rich and important woman she had always imagined herself to be.

She danced throughout the evening and enjoyed the attention of the guests. Her husband waited quietly in another room while she enjoyed the celebration.

Late at night, Mathilde and her husband returned home. When Mathilde looked in the mirror, she suddenly discovered that the diamond necklace was missing.

They searched everywhere—in her clothes, her pockets, and around the house—but they could not find it.

Monsieur Loisel went back along the route they had taken, hoping to find the necklace. He also contacted the police, cab companies, and various places where the necklace might have been found. But there was no news.

Mathilde and her husband decided that they must replace the necklace before telling Madame Forestier that it was lost.

They found a necklace that looked exactly like the borrowed one. However, it was extremely expensive. It cost 36,000 francs.

Monsieur Loisel had saved 18,000 francs from his father's inheritance. He borrowed the remaining money from different people, signing agreements and taking on heavy debts.

Finally, they bought the replacement necklace and gave it to Madame Forestier. She did not notice that it was a different necklace.

After that, Mathilde and her husband began a difficult life. They had to repay the huge debt.

They moved into a small apartment and dismissed their servant. Mathilde did all the household work herself. She cleaned the rooms, washed clothes, cooked meals, carried water, and went shopping.

Her husband worked during the day and did additional work at night to earn extra money.

For ten years, they lived a life of poverty and hardship. They worked continuously to repay the money they had borrowed.

The years of hard work changed Mathilde. The beautiful young woman who had once dreamed of luxury became tired, rough, and aged. She no longer cared about beautiful clothes or jewellery.

After ten years, they finally managed to repay all their debts.

One Sunday, Mathilde went for a walk along the Champs-Élysées. There she unexpectedly saw Madame Forestier.

Mathilde decided to tell her friend what had happened.

Madame Forestier was surprised by Mathilde's changed appearance. Mathilde explained that she had lost the necklace after the party and had spent ten years paying for a replacement.

Madame Forestier was shocked.

She told Mathilde that the original necklace had not been made of real diamonds. It was only an imitation necklace and had been worth about 500 francs.

Mathilde suddenly realized that she and her husband had spent ten years of their lives working to repay a huge debt for a necklace that was actually inexpensive.

The story ends with this painful irony: Mathilde's desire to appear rich and beautiful caused her to lose ten years of her life in poverty and hardship.

🌟 Moral : Be happy with what you have, not what you wish to have.
