THE TELL-TALE HEART

Title : The Tell-Tale Heart
Author : Edgar Allan Poe
Genre : Short Story, Gothic Fiction / Horror
Published : 1843
Theme : Guilt, madness, fear, crime
Characters : The Narrator, The Old Man, Three Police Officers

The story is narrated by an unnamed man who insists that he is not mad. He lives with an old man who has a strange, pale blue eye that frightens and disturbs him. The narrator says he does not hate the old man, but he becomes obsessed with the eye.

For several nights, the narrator secretly watches the old man while he sleeps. He carefully plans to kill him because of the eye.

One night, while watching him, the old man wakes up and becomes frightened. The narrator hears his heartbeat growing louder. Believing that the neighbours might hear it, the narrator attacks and kills the old man.

He then hides the body by cutting it into pieces and placing them under the floorboards. He feels proud because he believes he has committed the perfect crime.

Soon, three police officers arrive. A neighbour has heard a strange noise and reported it. The narrator confidently invites the officers inside and tells them that everything is fine.

The officers search the house but find nothing. The narrator becomes increasingly nervous. He imagines that he can hear the old man's heart beating beneath the floor.

The sound seems to grow louder and louder. Unable to bear his guilt and fear, the narrator finally confesses his crime and tells the police to tear up the floorboards.

The story ends with the narrator's confession, showing that his own guilt and fear reveal the crime he tried to hide.

🌟 Moral : Guilt cannot be hidden, even when the crime is hidden.
